# McKenna novelty theory and Timewave Zero

**Novelty theory** is Terence McKenna’s speculative cosmology in which **history** exhibits a **fractal ebb and flow** between **habit** (inertia, entropic closure) and **novelty** (connectivity, creative emergence). The **Timewave Zero** graph is the **visual output** of an algorithm claimed—by McKenna—to be **decoded from** the **King Wen sequence** of sixty-four hexagrams by treating **line-level transitions** between successive figures as **informatic differentials**, then **collapsing** that 384-line object through a **multi-scale fractal transform** (powers of 64) into a **time map**.

## Core claims (emic summary)

- The **King Wen order** is treated as a **conscious artifact** of ordering, not random permutation; McKenna emphasized **pairing rules** (invert / complement) and **global ratio** motifs (e.g. even vs. odd line-change statistics) as fingerprints of intent.
- **Conservation of novelty**: macroscopic “drain” toward terminal **zero** implies punctuated **ingression** of new configurations—language borrowed from **Whitehead** (“concrescence”) and **eschatology**.
- **Zero date**: McKenna anchored the graph’s global singularity to **2012-12-21**, asserting **independent convergence** with the **Maya Long Count** cycle end—**widely disputed** as methodology; see [[mckenna-timewave-mathematics-and-watkins-objection]].

## Archaeological caution (etic correction)

McKenna sometimes asserted extreme **antiquity** for the King Wen sequence (e.g. **Neolithic** animal-bone precedents framed as identical to the received order). **Mainstream sinology** does **not** support such identifications: early **oracle-bone** divination differs in **medium, grammar, and figure repertoire** from the mature **Zhou yi**; see [[king-wen-sequence]] and [[shanghai-museum-chu-bamboo-zhouyi]]. Treat those rhetorical chronologies as **folklore of reception**, not dating evidence.

## Software and afterlife

**Peter Meyer**’s implementations turned the graph into an **explorable UI** (“**Find Rhyme**” resonance searches, multi-scale zoom), embedding Timewave in **1990s cyberculture** and **2012** internet forums. Post-2012, the object survives as **digital heritage** and **open-source rewrites** rather than as predictive science.
