# Ten Wings (Yi zhuan)

The **Ten Wings** (十翼 *shí yì*; also **Yi zhuan** 易傳) are **ten commentary sections** canonized with the Zhou yi to form the received **Yijing**. They include **Tuan** (upper/lower), **Xiang** (Great and Small image wings), **Wényán** (focused on Qian and Kun), **Xì cí** (Appended Statements / “Great Commentary”), **Shuō guà**, **Xù guà**, and **Zá guà**. Linguistically they belong to a **later layer** than the oracle text; authorship was traditionally credited to **Confucius**, a view **abandoned** in serious philology since at least Song-dynasty skeptics and modern textual criticism.

## Why it matters

The Wings transform the Zhou yi from a **court and private mantic handbook** into a **cosmological and moral mirror** of the Han imperium—yin–yang, wuxing correspondences, and sage archetypes read back into the line statements.
