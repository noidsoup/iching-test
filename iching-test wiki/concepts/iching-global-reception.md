# Global reception — snapshots

This page collects **high-level landmarks** useful for cultural literacy, not a full bibliography.

- **1703**: Leibniz reads Jesuit-mediated hexagram diagrams and argues for **binary arithmetic** and theological universals (later critiqued by Hegel’s Eurocentric linguistics, then revisited by Derrida’s deconstructive reading of Hegel).
- **19th century**: Missionary and philological translations (Legge, etc.) place the Changes inside **Sacred Books of the East**-style orientalism.
- **1924 / 1950**: **Richard Wilhelm**’s German rendering and **Cary Baynes**’ English translation become the **counterculture standard**; **Carl Jung**’s foreword links hexagram imagery to **archetypes** and **synchronicity**.
- **20th century**: Artists and writers (Cage, Borges, Dick, Beatles-adjacent lyrics, etc.) treat the Yijing as a **chance-operation** or **symbolic oracle** source distinct from academic Yi learning.
- **1960s–1970s US/UK counterculture**: Wilhelm/Baynes as **Haight–Berkeley lingua franca**; Prankster-adjacent scenes; rock stars as **visibility multipliers** (see [[western-iching-counterculture-1960s-onward]]).
- **1970s–1990s psychedelic esoterica**: **Terence McKenna** re-encodes the **King Wen sequence** into **Timewave Zero** / **novelty theory** (*The Invisible Landscape*, 1975; software by **Peter Meyer**); see [[mckenna-novelty-theory-and-timewave-zero]] and [[mckenna-timewave-mathematics-and-watkins-objection]].
- **2012**: Popular fusion of **McKenna’s terminal date** with **Maya Long Count** memes—**post hoc** internet event, not a consensus of Maya studies or of Yijing philology.

Treat pop reception as **its own history**, partially disconnected from **Chinese philology** of the same decades (Shaughnessy, Gao Heng, Li Jingchi, new manuscript schools).
