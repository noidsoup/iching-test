# Western I Ching — counterculture (1960s onward)

From roughly the **1960s**, the Changes functioned in Anglophone culture less as a Qing-dynasty examination text than as a **portable oracle**, **Jungian mirror**, and **occult technology** sitting beside Tarot, astrology, and Eastern religion paperbacks. Distribution networks linked **Germanophone sinology** (Richard Wilhelm), **Swiss analytical psychology** (C. G. Jung’s foreword to the Baynes English translation), **American academic presses** (Princeton Bollingen, 1950/1967), and **Cold War bohemia** (Beats, Pranksters, rock musicians, science-fiction writers).

## Key phases

### 1950s–1960s: translation as cultural bridge

- **Wilhelm/Baynes** *I Ching or Book of Changes* (English 1950; Princeton hardcover **1967**) became the **default English shape** of the book: hexagrams plus interleaved **Wings** commentary, Jung’s foreword framing chance as **synchronicity**.
- **John Blofeld**’s Penguin-era translations (mid-1960s onward) competed on price and portability for travelers and seekers.
- Academic work on the **“traveling” Yijing** highlights **patronage chains** (Bollingen Foundation, Eranos circles, publishers) and **celebrity amplifiers** (Dylan, Dick, Harrison) as vectors of visibility—not as Confucian academy continuity.

### Late 1960s–1970s: Haight–Berkeley milieu

- Merry Pranksters–adjacent scenes treated the I Ching as a **cooler** divination deck than Tarot: three-coin throws, **“organic chance,”** anti-instrumentalist agency (see ethnographies of American **occulture**).
- **Rock and art**: Harrison–Shankar gift copies of Wilhelm/Baynes (1966) illustrate how **British Invasion** stars routed Eastern philosophy into mass media; **Pink Floyd**’s “Chapter 24” (1967) famously pastes Wilhelm/Baynes lines; the Beatles’ **“While My Guitar Gently Weeps”** (1968) is widely described as Harrison’s I Ching–inspired composition process (public narrative, not a scholarly edition of the classic).

### 1970s–1990s: psychedelia, systems culture, cyberpunk

- **Terence and Dennis McKenna** folded the **King Wen sequence** into **novelty theory** and **Timewave Zero** after the **1971 La Chorrera** expedition; *The Invisible Landscape* (1975) is the primary published technical–speculative statement.
- **Robert Anton Wilson**–adjacent readers networked Timewave with **Discordianism**, **eight-circuit model** discourse, and **fringe science** mailing lists.
- **Peter Meyer**’s MS-DOS implementations (**late 1980s–1990s**) turned Timewave into **software artifacts**, seeding **2012** anticipation cultures online.
- Parallel track: **Thomas Cleary**, **Richard John Lynn**, and others slowly pushed **academic translations** into bookstores, but counterculture uptake remained Wilhelm-tinted for decades.

### 2000s–2010s: internet eschatology and the 2012 node

- **December 21, 2012** fused **McKenna’s Timewave terminal** with **Maya Long Count** popularizations (separate lineages; McKenna claimed independent convergence).
- After 2012, Timewave discourse **forked**: archival software communities, **“resurrection”** hobby implementations, and retrospective **mathematical autopsy** essays.

### 2020s: survival as digital folklore

- GitHub reimplementations, YouTube explainers, and **McKenna Academy**–style preservation keep **novelty theory** alive as **digital heritage** rather than as academic Yijing philology.
