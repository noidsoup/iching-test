# Source notes — Shanghai Museum bamboo Zhou yi

Summary of Edward L. Shaughnessy’s **Early China** article introducing the **Shanghai Museum** purchased **Warring States Chu** bamboo manuscript of the **Zhou Yi** (English title along the lines of *A First Reading of the Shanghai Museum Bamboo-Strip Manuscript of the Zhou Yi*), as summarized in Cambridge Core metadata and secondary descriptions.

## Key points

- The manuscript is among texts acquired when the Shanghai Museum purchased **Chu-state bamboo books** from the Hong Kong market in **1994** (provenance via looting is the usual scholarly context; publication proceeded under Ma Chengyuan’s series).
- The Zhou yi portion occupies **58 strips**—roughly **one-third** of the received classic by strip count, not a complete consecutive copy.
- Paleographic and C-14 evidence published with the strips supports copying in **Chu around 300 BCE** (radiocarbon band cited in literature: roughly **306 ± 65 BCE** in one reported determination).
- Orthography and wording align **closely with the received Zhou yi** while preserving **minor variants** typical of pre-unification manuscript culture.
- Unusual **marking symbols** (red/black notations) appear after some hexagram names and at ends of sections; their exact mantic function is debated.

## Detailed notes

Cross-comparisons in scholarship routinely align this witness with **Mawangdui silk Yijing**, **Guodian** materials, **Baoshan** and other Chu divination corpora to map how stable the hexagram statements were before Qin–Han standardization.

Publication line: *Shanghai bowuguan cang Zhanguo Chu zhushu* volumes (2001 onward); Zhou yi volume is especially consulted for photographs, transcriptions, and parallel columns to received text and Mawangdui.
